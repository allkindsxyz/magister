From the authors' notes

The Deck's Mythology

Four worlds in fifty-four faces

Magister is a full 54-card art deck: paintings first, then cards. Suits are not decorative trim — each one is a realm with its own laws, cast, and symbolism. Ranks and houses are woven into the painting itself: you read the card by looking, not by corner indices.

Ann and Scarab

Hearts — Olympian gods

The classical pantheon in motion: desire, metamorphosis, oracle and flame. Hearts carry mythic episodes where mortals and immortals negotiate fate.

♦

Diamonds — Bearers of secrets

Alchemical emissaries, hidden orders, and whispers behind crowns. Diamonds track those who traffic in knowledge that should not travel in plain sight.

Templar's Hunt

♣

Clubs — Knights Templar

Crusade steel, lost treasure-maps, and the long shadow of the order. Clubs anchor the deck in medieval intrigue and the arc from devotion to legend.

Michael and the Chameleon

♠

Spades — Celebrities & icons

Modern myth-making: faces the world thought it knew, reframed as archetypes. Spades ask what happens when public masks become permanent emblems.

Every illustration is a small narrative — portrait, emblem, and historical echo at once. Text on the site expands the frame; the cardboard remains the primary grimoire.

The authors

Fifty-four paintings. Fifty-four stories.

The cards are not illustrations of a game. They are finished paintings, then cut to the rhythm of a deck. Alexander Zhilchik authored the project and the world. Vasil Pochitsky — signing as Dr.Klein — brought the pictorial language: the hand on every one of the fifty-four.

    Arthive profile

From canvas to card

Research, paint, then the deck

Two years from the first brief to the finished edition — every card earned its place as a painting before it became cardboard.

Zhilchik locked the cast, intrigues, and four suits as worlds. Pochitsky built each image on archives, emblem books, and alchemical sources — long research before the first mark.

Studies were not disposable thumbnails: every one was pushed toward a finished easel piece — composition, light, surface as standalone art. Only then was the work fitted to the scale of a playing card.

What you hold is a gallery object you can also deal: stories in paint, compressed into fifty-four faces.

Play sketches

Systems in draft

Four table practices sketched on this deck. They are not a finished line and not what we are shopping as a complete game. The asset is the world and the art; play can be built with the right partner.

I

Play I

The Procession

A trick-taking game for two to five players. The figures are ranked not by number but by the weight of what they carry. The rules are simple. The logic is not.

Players: 2 – 5 · Duration: 40 – 90 min
II

Play II

The Market of Wonders

Trade and bluff with the figures of the deck. Each figure has a price; each price is a negotiation; each negotiation is a small lie told politely.

Players: 3 – 6 · Duration: 30 – 60 min
III

Play III

The Oracle's Method

A solo practice. Lay out the figures in silence. Study their burdens. Ask your question. The cards do not answer — but something shifts.

Players: 1 · Duration: As long as needed
IV

Play IV

The Duel

The classic duel. Fast, tense, played in silence. Know when your figure can be matched. Know when to walk away. Walking away is sometimes the only move.

Players: 2 · Duration: 15 – 30 min
